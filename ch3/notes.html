Chapter 3: Insurance Products and related services


Topic 1: Characteristics of Insurance Products in the Saudi Market

  • Diverse and Miscellaneous Products

    • Insurance products are varied to meet the different needs of individuals and companies.

  • Standardized Offerings

    • Many insurance products in the Saudi market are similar to those in other international markets. This is because they are developed by industry experts to address common risks faced by people and businesses globally.

  • Flexibility and Customization

    • Insurance products are designed to be flexible. An applicant for insurance can select the specific coverages that match the risks they face, or they can add optional coverages to a standard policy to enhance their protection.

  • Licensed and Regulated Products

    • Every insurance product sold in Saudi Arabia must be licensed by the Saudi Central Bank (SAMA).

    • An insurance company cannot market or sell any product without first obtaining this license.

    • The licensing process involves submitting a detailed file with all required information to SAMA, which may grant a temporary or permanent license for the product.

  • Technologically Accessible

    • Insurance products are increasingly keeping pace with technology. Customers can now purchase some types of insurance directly through company websites without needing to visit a physical branch.

Topic 2: Insurance Products for Individuals

  • A. Comprehensive Motor Insurance

    • This policy covers damage to an individual's own vehicle.

    • Section 1: Own Damage: This section covers accidental damage to the vehicle's body from events like collisions, overturning, fire, theft, and natural hazards. It also covers deliberate damage caused by others.

    • Section 2: Third-Party Liability: This section covers the policyholder's legal responsibility to pay for damages they cause to others. The insurance company will pay compensation up to a maximum of SAR 10 million for property damage, bodily injury, or death caused by the insured vehicle.

    • Optional Extensions:

      • Personal Accident: For an additional premium, this extends coverage to the driver and/or passengers for death or disability resulting from an accident.

      • Geographical Extension: This allows the policy's coverage to be extended to other countries, such as those in the GCC, Jordan, or Egypt.

  • B. Motor Insurance Third-Party Liability (Compulsory)

    • This is a mandatory insurance policy in Saudi Arabia, governed by a unified policy issued by SAMA.

    • It covers the policyholder's legal liability for damages caused to third parties (people who are not a party to the insurance contract).

    • Coverage includes:

      • Physical injuries to third parties, whether they are inside or outside the vehicle.

      • Damage to the property of third parties.

  • C. Medical Insurance for Individuals

    • Individuals can voluntarily purchase medical insurance for themselves and their families.

    • Benefits typically include:

      • Outpatient and inpatient treatment, including hospital accommodation.

      • Dental and vision care.

      • Pregnancy and childbirth expenses.

      • Preventive care like vaccinations.

      • Treatment for acute psychological conditions, Alzheimer's, and autism.

      • Costs for organ harvesting from donors.

      • Treatment for extreme obesity (gastric sleeve surgery) in specific cases.

  • D. Medical Malpractice Insurance

    • This policy protects medical practitioners against claims of negligence or errors made during their work.

    • It is a mandatory coverage for doctors to obtain their license from the health authorities in KSA.

    • The coverage is not limited to doctors and surgeons but can also include paramedics, nurses, pharmacists, and medical technicians.

  • E. Homeowners Insurance

    • This policy protects a homeowner's property against a range of risks.

    • It can cover the building, its contents (like furniture and personal belongings), or both.

    • Covered perils typically include fire, natural disasters, explosions, earthquakes, riots, and theft.

  • F. International Travel Insurance

    • This policy provides coverage for losses that may occur while traveling abroad.

    • It can cover emergency medical expenses, trip cancellation, lost baggage, and personal accidents.

    • This coverage is often a mandatory requirement for obtaining a travel visa to certain countries.

  • G. Personal Accident Insurance

    • This policy provides financial compensation to the insured person (or their heirs) if they suffer an injury, disability, or death due to an accident.

    • Coverage is typically worldwide and valid for the entire insurance period.

  • H. Hajj and Umrah Insurance

    • These policies are designed to compensate pilgrims for losses suffered during their journey and stay for Hajj or Umrah.

    • Coverage can include:

      • Medical care for accidents or illness.

      • Costs of emergency medical transportation.

      • Repatriation of the body in case of death.

      • Costs related to flight delays or cancellations.

      • For Umrah, coverage has been added for risks associated with Covid-19, including treatment and quarantine costs.

  • I. Domestic Labor Insurance

    • This policy covers risks related to domestic workers.

    • Benefits can include:

      • Repatriation Costs: Covering the expense of returning a worker to their home country in cases of escape, contract cancellation, or death.

      • Disability: Compensating the worker for permanent or temporary disability due to a work-related accident.

      • Replacement Costs: Reimbursing the sponsor for the costs of hiring a new worker if the original worker refuses to work, has a health issue preventing them from working, or escapes.

  • J. Protection and Savings Insurance

    • These policies provide a financial payout in the event of the insured's death or disability, or at the end of an agreed-upon period.

    • Protection Insurance (Term Life): Pays a specified amount to beneficiaries if the insured dies or becomes disabled during the contract term.

    • Protection and Savings Insurance (Endowment/Investment-Linked): Combines a death benefit with a savings component, paying out an amount, including investment returns, at a future date.

Topic 3: Insurance Products for Corporations

  • A. Motor Fleet Insurance

    • Provides insurance solutions for companies with fleets of vehicles of various sizes and types, including leased vehicles, rental cars, and commercial transport trucks.

    • Companies can choose between comprehensive coverage (covering own damage and third-party liability) or third-party liability only.

  • B. Medical Insurance for Companies

    • This is the standard group health insurance that employers provide for their employees, following the regulations set by the Council of Cooperative Health Insurance.

    • It typically includes coverage for outpatient clinics, hospital stays, pregnancy, and dental care, with an annual limit per person (e.g., SAR 500,000).

  • C. Protection and Savings Insurance (Group)

    • Credit Life Insurance: This program is for banks and finance companies. It insures borrowers, and if a borrower dies or becomes permanently disabled, the insurance company pays off the outstanding loan balance to the financial institution.

    • Group Term Life Insurance: This is for employers to provide a death and disability benefit for their employees. If a covered employee dies or becomes permanently disabled, the policy pays an agreed-upon sum to the employee or their beneficiaries.

  • D. Property Insurance

    • Fire and Allied Perils: This policy compensates for accidental damage to property from perils like fire and lightning. It can be extended to cover other risks such as explosions, storms, floods, earthquakes, and riots.

    • Property All Risks: This offers more comprehensive coverage for commercial and industrial properties, covering all risks except for those that are specifically excluded in the policy.

    • Consequential Loss (Business Interruption): This is a crucial addition to a property policy. It covers the loss of profits and fixed expenses that a business incurs when its operations are interrupted due to a physical damage event (like a fire).

  • E. Engineering Insurance

    • This is a specialized category of insurance for construction and engineering projects.

    • Contractor's All Risks (CAR): Provides comprehensive protection for engineering projects like buildings and bridges against all risks, unless specifically excluded. Can be extended to include third-party liability.

    • Contractor's Plant and Machinery (CPM): Covers sudden and accidental damage to construction machinery and equipment used on a worksite.

    • Electronic Equipment Insurance: Covers damage to computers, medical devices, and other electronic information processing equipment.

    • Machinery Breakdown: Covers unexpected damage to insured machines due to issues like design errors, faulty installation, or lack of skill in operation.

    • Inherent Defect Insurance: A mandatory 10-year policy covering hidden structural defects in buildings that are discovered after the building is occupied.

  • F. Liability Insurance

    • Professional Liability (Errors & Omissions): Protects professionals like architects, engineers, lawyers, and accountants against claims arising from negligence or errors in their professional services.

    • Public Liability: Covers a business's legal liability for accidental bodily injury or property damage to third parties that arises from its operations.

    • Product Liability: Protects manufacturers, distributors, and sellers against legal liability for bodily injury or property damage caused by a defect in a product they sold.

    • Workmen's Compensation: Under Saudi Labor Law, employers are liable to compensate employees for death, injury, or illness resulting from work accidents. This policy provides the funds to meet this legal obligation.

    • Money Insurance: Covers the loss of money, checks, and securities while they are on the business's premises or in transit.

    • Fidelity Guarantee Insurance: Protects a business from financial losses caused by acts of fraud or dishonesty committed by its own employees, such as cashiers or accountants.

    • Directors' and Officers' (D&O) Liability: Provides coverage for company directors and managers against liability for errors or negligence committed in the course of managing the company's affairs.

  • G. Marine Insurance

    • This policy covers loss or damage to goods during transport by sea, air, or road, whether for domestic, import, or export shipments.

  • H. Energy Insurance

    • This is a highly specialized type of insurance for the oil, gas, petrochemical, and electricity sectors. It covers risks like fire, explosions, and destruction of facilities, and can be extended to include liability and business interruption.

  • I. Aviation Insurance

    • Aircraft Hull and Liability: Covers physical damage to the aircraft itself, as well as legal liability for injury or damage caused to third parties (including passengers).

    • Pilot Loss of License: Compensates a pilot or crew member if their license is withdrawn (temporarily or permanently) due to medical unfitness resulting from an accident or illness.

  • J. Other Specialized Insurances

    • Drone Insurance: Covers risks resulting from the use of licensed drones for commercial purposes like surveys, inspections, and aerial photography.

    • Aquaculture Insurance: Covers losses to fish farms resulting from risks like disease, climate change (e.g., high temperatures), theft, and water pollution.

    • Event Cancellation Insurance: Provides financial protection to organizers of licensed events (like conferences, festivals, and sporting events) against losses if the event has to be cancelled, postponed, or relocated for reasons beyond their control, such as severe weather.

Topic 4: Principles of Customer Service in the Insurance Market

  • Insurance Market Code of Conduct Regulations

    • SAMA has issued these regulations to establish high standards for all insurance companies and professionals when dealing with customers.

    • Failure to comply can result in penalties. The regulations are built on several key principles:

  • A. Integrity

    • Companies must act honestly, fairly, and transparently in all their dealings and fulfill all their obligations to customers.

  • B. Skill, Care, and Diligence

    • Companies must operate with professional skill and the utmost care to serve their customers effectively.

  • C. Non-Discrimination

    • Companies cannot unfairly discriminate against customers and must provide valid reasons for rejecting or cancelling a policy. SAMA has specifically emphasized that insurance services cannot be denied to persons with disabilities.

  • D. Data Protection

    • Companies have a strict duty to protect customers' personal data. Data must be kept secure, used only for its intended purpose, and cannot be disclosed to a third party without permission (with exceptions for auditors and actuaries).

  • E. Protection of Customer Funds

    • Any premium collected by a broker or agent must be deposited into a separate, independent bank account and cannot be treated as the broker's or agent's own property.

  • F. Conflict of Interest

    • Companies must take steps to identify and manage any conflicts of interest, always putting the customer's interest first.

  • G. Clarity in Policy Documents

    • Insurance policies must be written in clear, simple language (in Arabic, with an English version permitted) and printed in a readable text size.

    • The policy must clearly state all key information, including the policy number, coverage period, description of cover, limits, deductibles, conditions, and exclusions.

  • H. "Free Look" Period for Protection & Savings

    • For Protection and Savings policies, there is a mandatory "Free Look" period of at least 21 days from when the customer receives the policy.

    • This gives the customer time to review the policy and return it for a refund if it is not suitable (less any costs incurred by the insurer, like for a medical exam).

  • I. Pre-Sale Communication and Advice

    • Companies must gather enough information from customers to assess their needs properly and recommend suitable products.

    • Brokers must make a reasonable effort to get quotes from several insurers and justify why they are recommending a particular one, especially if it is not the cheapest option.

  • J. Post-Sale Services

    • Claims Settlement: Companies must handle claims promptly and fairly. They must acknowledge receipt of a claim, keep the customer informed of its progress (at least every 15 days), and settle the claim within specified timeframes (e.g., 15 days for individuals, 45 days for companies).

    • Complaints Handling: Companies must have a fair and transparent process for handling complaints, acknowledge them, and resolve them promptly (e.g., within 10 working days).

    • Renewal and Expiry: Companies must inform customers in advance of their policy's renewal or expiry date to give them time to arrange continuous coverage. For vehicle insurance, this is often done via a mandatory text message.

  • K. Distribution of Surplus

    • Because all insurance in KSA is cooperative, insurers must have a SAMA-approved mechanism for distributing any underwriting surplus back to policyholders. This mechanism must be available to the public.
